# DM542T driver settings

The DM542T is configured entirely by DIP switches. These settings MUST match
`machine/wen33013.toml`; when they disagree, the machine moves the wrong
distance and nothing reports an error.

Source: StepperOnline DM542T(V4.0) manual.

## Hard limits (why the constraint checker exists)

| Parameter | Limit |
|---|---|
| Minimum PUL low time | **2.5 us** |
| Direction setup before PUL | **5.0 us** |
| Maximum pulse frequency | **200 kHz** |
| Output current range | 1.0 - 4.5 A (8 DIP settings) |
| Microstep range | 200 - 25,600 steps/rev (16 DIP settings) |

Exceeding the timing limits does **not** produce an error. The driver drops
pulses and the axis quietly loses position - which is the entire reason
`tools/machinegen/constraints.py` refuses to generate a config that would do
it, and the reason the belt encoders (ADR-0005) exist to catch it when it
happens anyway.

## Current - SW1, SW2, SW3

Set to the motor's rated current, not higher. Our configured value is
`driver.current_a` in the TOML (**PROVISIONAL 2.8 A** until the motors are in
hand and their nameplate is read).

Peak and RMS are different numbers on these drives - read the table printed on
the drive itself and match the RMS column to the motor rating.

## Microstep - SW5, SW6, SW7, SW8

Our configured value is 32x microstepping -> **6400 steps/rev**.

With a 5 mm ballscrew lead that gives **1280 steps/mm** (0.78 um per
microstep). At the configured 41.67 mm/s this demands 53.3 kHz, comfortably
inside the 200 kHz ceiling, with ~9.4 us pulses against a 2.5 us floor.

Read the microstep table printed on the drive and set the four switches to the
row showing 6400. Do not assume another drive's table matches - the clones
vary.

## SW4

Half-current / standstill-current selection. For a mill, prefer **full
current at standstill**: a Z axis holding a head needs its torque when it is
not moving. Confirm the sense of this switch against the printed table, as it
is inverted on some units.

## Wiring notes

- PUL and DIR are opto-isolated inputs, typically driven in common-anode
  configuration from the RP2040's 3.3 V outputs through series resistors. The
  RP2040 is **3.3 V**; confirm the resistor value for 3.3 V rather than
  reusing a 5 V figure, or the opto will be underdriven and steps will be
  marginal in a way that is very hard to diagnose.
- ENA is usually best left disconnected. Wiring it and getting the polarity
  wrong leaves the drive disabled with every light looking correct.
- Run step/dir pairs away from motor leads. These drives are noisy and the
  belt encoder cabling shares the enclosure.

## Commissioning checklist

Before an axis is allowed to move under power, see `/commission-axis`.

- [ ] DIP current matches the motor nameplate
- [ ] DIP microstep reads 6400 on this drive's own table
- [ ] `make check` clean
- [ ] Measured 50 mm travel agrees with commanded to within 0.05 mm
